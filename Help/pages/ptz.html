PTZ Camera & Auto Frame - Setup Guide

▶ Watch: Advanced PTZ setup (video tutorial)

1. OBS hotkey setup (required first)

The PTZ overlay arrows and Auto Frame both work by sending hotkey commands through OBS WebSocket using TriggerHotkeyByKeySequence. OBS must have these keys registered as hotkeys or the buttons appear but do nothing.
If the keyboard Arrow keys are NOT set in OBS, the PTZ overlay arrows will NOT move the camera.
  1. In OBS open File → Settings → Hotkeys (or press Ctrl+,).
  2. In the search box type the name of your PTZ source (e.g. "PTZ Controls", "VISCA", "NDI PTZ", or the source name you gave your camera).
  3. You will see entries like: Pan Left / Pan Right, Tilt Up / Tilt Down, Zoom In / Zoom Out.
  4. Click the field next to each action and press the key:
    • Tilt Up → press Arrow Up
    • Tilt Down → press Arrow Down
    • Pan Left → press Arrow Left
    • Pan Right → press Arrow Right
    • Zoom In → press Numpad +
    • Zoom Out → press Numpad -
  5. Click Apply then OK.
  6. Test: hover the OBS preview in this app, hold an arrow button - the camera should now move.
Each hotkey must be unique in OBS (no conflicts). If another source/filter already uses Arrow keys, OBS will trigger both - remove conflicts or use modifier keys.

2. PTZ overlay arrows (hover the preview)

When you move the mouse over the OBS preview panel, four directional arrows and two zoom buttons appear:
  • ▲ Tilt Up - hold to tilt camera up continuously
  • ▼ Tilt Down - hold to tilt camera down
  • ◄ Pan Left - hold to pan camera left
  • ► Pan Right - hold to pan camera right
  • + Zoom In - hold to zoom in
  • − Zoom Out - hold to zoom out
Release the button to stop movement. The arrows disappear when the mouse leaves the preview.
Common issue: arrows visible but camera doesn't move → Arrow keys aren't assigned as OBS hotkeys yet (see Section 1), or the PTZ camera source isn't connected/active in OBS. Check OBS's Help → Log Files for hotkey errors.

3. Auto Frame (AF: OFF / AF: ON) - click to center

Auto Frame is a click-to-center tool. You click on the face in the preview and the camera tilts to bring that point to the vertical centre of the frame.
  1. Click [AF: OFF] to enable → button turns green [AF: ON].
  2. The cursor changes to a crosshair when you hover over the OBS preview panel (inside the green ROI box).
  3. Move the crosshair over the speaker's nose or eyes.
  4. Click once → the camera tilts up or down to bring that exact point to the vertical centre.
  5. Repeat any time the speaker moves or changes position.
  6. If the camera movement is too small or too large, adjust the Camera Speed using the [−] and [+] buttons (S:80). Increase S: if the camera barely moves; decrease S: if it overshoots past the target.
Notes: Auto Frame only activates on the configured "Speaker" scene. The green ROI box (Section 4) defines the clickable area. Speed (Section 5) controls how far each click moves the camera. OBS WebSocket must be connected and Arrow Up/Down hotkeys must be set in OBS (Section 1) - otherwise clicks do nothing.

4. Set ROI - Region of Interest

The ROI (green rectangle) defines the clickable area for Auto Frame. The crosshair cursor is only active when your mouse is inside the ROI box - clicks outside it do nothing.
  1. Click [Set ROI] → green box appears, button shows [ROI: ON].
  2. Drag the box left/right to centre it over the speaker's horizontal position in the preview.
  3. Drag the side handles to adjust the width - narrow it to just cover the column where the speaker stands or sits.
  4. Click [Set ROI] again to confirm and hide the editor.
Shape the box correctly - make it tall and narrow, not square. Narrow horizontally (there's no left/right pan, only tilt), but make it as tall as possible (it can extend above/below the visible preview edge - that's fine). A taller box gives you a bigger vertical click target, from head to waist level.
Tip: you can adjust the ROI while AF is ON without disabling it. Re-set the ROI if the speaker's position on screen changes or if you switch to a camera with a different framing.

5. Camera speed (− S:XX +)

The speed value controls how many PTZ pulses are sent per Auto Frame correction step.
  • Range: 10-200 pulses per correction.
  • Lower: slower, more precise, less jitter.
  • Higher: faster, larger corrections, may overshoot.
  • Default: 80 (good starting point for most cameras).
Adjust with [−] and [+] (steps of 10). The current value is shown as S:80 between the buttons.
S:20-40 Very slow cameras or fine-tuning only.
S:50-90 Most PTZ cameras at medium speed preset.
S:100+ Fast cameras or when large corrections are needed.

6. Step-by-step first-time setup

  1. Install PTZ plugin in OBS - download "obs-ptz" (PTZ Controls) from OBS Forums/GitHub, or configure your VISCA-over-IP / NDI PTZ source in OBS. Add a PTZ camera source to your OBS scene.
  2. Assign Arrow hotkeys in OBS - OBS → Settings → Hotkeys, assign Arrow Up/Down/Left/Right and Numpad +/- as in Section 1, then Apply and close Settings.
  3. Connect this app to OBS WebSocket - in this app open Settings → OBS WebSocket, enter the host (127.0.0.1) and port (default 4456), click Connect - status indicator should turn green.
  4. Test the PTZ arrows - hover the OBS preview panel in this app, hold an arrow - camera should tilt/pan. If nothing moves, re-check Step 2 (hotkeys in OBS).
  5. Configure Auto Frame - switch OBS Program scene to "Speaker", the AF controls appear, click [Set ROI] and position the green box, set speed (start at S:80), click [AF: OFF] to enable, hover inside the green ROI and click on the speaker's nose or eyes to centre the camera.
Reminder: Arrow keys must be set as OBS hotkeys (Step 2) before the PTZ overlay arrows or Auto Frame will work.
